What am I looking at? (the physics, briefly)

For a cello string to vibrate cleanly (in "Helmholtz motion"), the bow force has to sit within a range that depends on bow speed and how far from the bridge you bow (the sounding point). Too little force and the bow slips too easily — you get a thin, breathy surface sound / whistle. Too much force and the string sticks and slips multiple times per cycle — a harsh crunch. This relationship is classically summarized by the Schelleng diagram: a wedge-shaped region of usable force that widens with bow speed and narrows as you bow further from the bridge.

This app uses a simplified, pedagogically-tuned version of that relationship (not a precise acoustic simulation) to make the trends explorable and audible.

About the wrist sensor connection

Live streaming uses the Web Bluetooth API, which requires Chrome or Edge (desktop or Android) over HTTPS or localhost — it isn't available on iOS at all. WitMotion sensors don't use a single standardized GATT UUID across every model, so this app ships with the commonly used WT901BLE/BWT901BLE values and falls back to scanning for any notifying characteristic; if it can't find your sensor's data channel automatically, use the Advanced UUID fields once you've found them with a generic BLE scanner app.

About the tuner

Pitch detection uses your microphone and runs entirely in the browser (audio never leaves your device). It needs HTTPS or localhost, same as the Bluetooth sensor above. Detection uses autocorrelation on the raw waveform, tuned for the cello's range (roughly 55–1400 Hz) — very quiet or noisy input, or multiple simultaneous notes (double stops/chords), won't track reliably since this only detects a single pitch at a time.
